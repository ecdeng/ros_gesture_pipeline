## Tilt platform: how hard a hand can push before the head yields

In current-based position mode the servo gives way instead of fighting once its current cap is reached. This is the force at the crown (h 198) at which the first servo reaches its cap, on top of gravity at rest.

| Push | 300 mA idle | 400 mA idle | 600 mA motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Backward, at the crown | 1.9 N (195 gf) | 2.5 N (259 gf) | 3.8 N (389 gf) |
| Sideways, at the crown | 1.1 N (113 gf) | 1.8 N (187 gf) | 3.3 N (337 gf) |
| Straight down, on the crown | 5.3 N (537 gf) | 10.6 N (1078 gf) | 21.2 N (2161 gf) |

A light pet is about 1–3 N. At 300 mA a 1 N sideways touch already moves the head, so the stroke detector would fight the compliance; 400 mA roughly doubles that threshold while still yielding to a firm press. A push harder than this back-drives the plastic gears slowly; it does not need to be resisted.

## Assumptions to replace with measurements

- Fur areal mass 450 g/m², foam 300 g/m² (10 mm of 30 kg/m³). Weigh a 10 × 10 cm swatch of each.
- Skin and skirt drag on tilt: 0.03 N·m per axis. Measure with the skirt-drag test on the P1 rig (current with and without a taped fur strip).
- Arm fur-hinge resistance 0.03 N·m and arm mass 22 g at 25 mm. Measure by hanging a weight on a sewn arm.
- Servo current not producing torque (I0) = 0.10 A. Read Present Current with the horn free to find it.
- Yaw friction 0.08 N·m at the puck, dominated by the sealed bearing (a shielded ZZ bearing drags far less).
- Torque scales with supply voltage; these numbers are at 5.0 V at the servo.
